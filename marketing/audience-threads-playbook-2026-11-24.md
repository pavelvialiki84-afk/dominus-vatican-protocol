## 2. Where to find readers, and what counts as high intent

| Source | Typically hot intent? | Approach / constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon category/search/product page | Yes, actively selecting a book | Accurate metadata, cover, sample, price; do not assert checkout verified unless observed |
| Reader asks for a Vatican/AI thriller recommendation | Yes | Contextual one-to-one recommendation only when invited |
| Reader book clubs and genre recommendation threads | Medium-to-hot | Participate substantively, respect no-promo rules, no unsolicited mass posting |
| Facebook genre groups | Mixed | Personalized responses and approved promo windows; distinguish engagement from sales |
| Book reviewers/bloggers | Medium | Genuine outreach with clear fit; request honest reviews, no compensation contingent on favorable rating |
| Threads literary discussions | Usually cold-to-warm | Question-first discussion and thoughtful replies, occasional chapter link |
| Instagram Reels and Stories | Usually discovery | 1 visual hook → defined story stakes → sample link; measure site traffic |
| Author website, chapter preview | Warm | Language-specific value, legible mobile text, actual source excerpts |
| AI/theology/history topic communities | Cold-to-warm | Valuable discussion without misrepresenting the work as a real archive disclosure |
| Direct messages | Only when reader initiated | No spam, no contact scraping |

**Hot** = visitor explicitly seeks a book or clicks a retail destination; **warm** = voluntarily reads a sample/explores dossier; **cold** = sees a debate post. These are working operational labels, not person-level tracking.

## 3. Ten genuine buying or interest triggers per audience type

Adapt only triggers with a clear causal fit for books. The 10 events below are *hypotheses*, to be tested by voluntary discussions and aggregate traffic.

1. Reader finishes a comparable genre novel and seeks the next book.
2. Reader asks for AI-related speculative fiction.
3. Book club requests an accessible debate on human identity/agency.
4. Reader selects a new Kindle device or switches to ebooks (format discovery).
5. A community publishes a themed Vatican-thriller recommendation request.
6. Reader requests novels about archives, manuscripts or historical mysteries.
7. New conversation about artificial intelligence reconstructing human voices.
8. Reader specifically searches for an original-language edition.
9. Reader previews Book One and wants to continue the chapter.
10. Reader of Book One asks whether another installment is actually available.

**Do not treat** release gossip, sensational claims about actual Vatican discoveries or fabricated urgency as purchase triggers.

## 5. Five quick qualification questions (conversations only)

Use naturally, **not** as a forced form, and do not collect personal/sensitive data.

1. “Do you prefer mysteries, technological thrillers, or historical suspense?”
2. “Would you rather read a short preview first, or look at the book's premise?”
3. “Which language do you prefer for reading fiction?”
4. “Kindle/ebook or print?”
5. “Which part interests you most: the archive, AI, or the philosophical question?”

After one or two answers, send a **single appropriate link**, never bombard with five markets. No retargeting from sensitive religion disclosures.

## 6. Threads operating system — experiment, not dogma

The screenshot claims 3–10 posts/day “works”; this is **unverified advice** and should not become an automatic spam quota. Test **1–2 original topical posts/day** on days when existing Metricool slots allow; reserve time for **3–5 useful, genuinely relevant replies**. Avoid overposting identical topics across languages without audience evidence. The current queue already has **18 Threads assignments** through 8 December.

**Post mix as initial hypothesis:**
- 45% substantial dialogue starters (AI testimony, evidence, authority; no link unless relevant).
- 30% story-led micro-fiction hooks grounded in the actual book; optional sample link.
- 15% author perspective (archival/biblical/historical research ethics; carefully separate fact from fiction).
- 10% direct edition CTA, only with genuinely available marketplace and sample.

**Post construction:** meaningful hook in first 1–2 lines → one sharp, understandable thought → one focused question or single CTA. Avoid fake claims (“actual Vatican file discovered”), overwrought clickbait, jargon overload, duplicate posts, copied Threads, reply farming and forced mutual follows.

**Profile and pinned post:** Clearly name *Pavel Velichii — author of DOMINUS*; describe fictional thriller and multilingual editions; link to `https://dominusbooks.com/editions.html` or the book's main landing. A separate Telegram funnel is **optional**, not automatically worthwhile: only establish it after an opt-in audience exists, the publishing capacity is available, and channel-specific subscription benefits are concrete.

## 8. Weekly funnel: data → weakest step → one test

Measure only independently evidenced values:
1. **Reach:** impressions/posts by provider (from Metricool if supported), not raw profile view claims.
2. **Interest:** likes/replies/saves with context; no engagement = buyer assumption.
3. **Website arrival:** total visits, pageviews by path, traffic source (Metricool website).
4. **Sampling:** chapter pageviews as *proxy*; reading completion unknown unless measured.
5. **Storefront click:** not currently measurable merely by conditional dataLayer event; must connect and validate analytics consumer first.
6. **Paid order:** retailer/KDP report only. No attribution to a post unless actual UTM/order-level evidence available.
7. **Revenue:** paid royalties, after returns and timing, never estimated from clicks.
