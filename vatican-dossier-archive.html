THE VATICAN DOSSIER · Reader Companion

A Death in the Vatican Archive: Where the Fiction Begins

The first mystery of DOMINUS — The Vatican Protocol begins in a place built to preserve memory. The contradiction is deliberate: what happens when a record is recovered but nobody can establish who created it?

The room before the revelation

On 12 June 2035, at 03:17, Brother Salvatore Ventresca enters a restricted vault of the Vatican Apostolic Archive. The room is cold, the locks are both mechanical and electronic, and a night patrol is approaching. He carries an old wooden casket bearing an inscription: Veritas ex algorithmo — “Truth from the algorithm.”

These details are not simply decoration. They place the reader between two kinds of custody: the physical archive, where provenance and access matter, and a computational system, where information can appear without a clear human chain of responsibility.

A document without a history

The story turns on an apparently impossible record — a file whose existence cannot be accounted for by the system's creation logs. The anomaly invites a question familiar to historians and archivists: is a document credible merely because it can be read?

Authenticity, in both archives and investigations, depends on more than persuasive content. The route by which information was created, stored, altered, and transmitted matters. A record with no demonstrable provenance is a dramatic device precisely because it undermines the normal basis of trust.

Why the archive matters

DOMINUS is not a documentary about the Holy See, nor does it claim to reveal a real Vatican technology. It uses a fictional future to explore something real: the modern difficulty of distinguishing an authoritative record from a convincing reconstruction.

The thriller begins with a death. Its deeper mystery is who will be permitted to interpret the evidence left behind.
